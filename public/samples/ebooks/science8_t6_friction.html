Unit: Forces, Friction & Pressures

Friction: Causes and Effects

Understanding surface interlocking, types of friction, advantages, disadvantages, and drag reduction.

What Causes Friction?

Friction is a force that opposes the relative motion between two surfaces in contact. It always acts in the direction opposite to the applied force. Friction is caused by the interlocking of microscopic irregularities (hills and valleys) on the two surfaces.

⚙️

Smooth vs. Rough Surfaces

No surface is perfectly smooth. Rough surfaces have larger and more numerous irregularities, causing stronger interlocking and therefore higher frictional resistance.

Four Types of Friction

Friction varies depending on whether an object is stationary, moving, rolling, or in a fluid:

Type Strength Description
1. Static Friction Highest Acts on objects that are resting on a surface. Opposes initial motion.
2. Sliding Friction Medium Acts when one object slides over another. Slightly weaker than static.
3. Rolling Friction Lowest (Solid) Acts when an object rolls on wheels or rollers over another surface.
4. Fluid Friction (Drag) Variable The resistive force exerted by fluids (liquids and gases) on objects.

Friction: A Necessary Evil

✅ Advantages

- Allows us to walk without slipping.
- Enables brakes to stop vehicles.
- Helps write with a pen/pencil on paper.
- Enables nails/screws to hold in walls.

❌ Disadvantages

- Wears out shoe soles and machinery parts.
- Generates unwanted heat in engines.
- Causes energy loss by opposing movement, requiring more fuel.
