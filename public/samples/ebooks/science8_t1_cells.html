Unit: Cellular & Microscopic Life

Cell Structures & Organelles

Understanding plant and animal cell components, membranes, vacuoles, and nuclei functions.

The Basic Unit of Life

In biological terms, a cell is defined as the smallest unit that is capable of performing life processes. English scientist Robert Hooke first discovered cells in 1665 using a primitive microscope. Today, we know cells contain specialized units called organelles, each carrying out unique tasks vital to the cell's survival.

🧬

Quick Fact: The Cell Theory

Formulated by Schleiden, Schwann, and Virchow, the cell theory states: (1) All living organisms are made of one or more cells, (2) The cell is the basic unit of life, and (3) All cells arise from pre-existing cells.

Plant Cells vs. Animal Cells

While both plant and animal cells are eukaryotic (possessing a membrane-bound nucleus), they have key structural differences reflecting their lifestyle and nutritional needs:

Organelle / Feature Plant Cell Animal Cell
Cell Wall Present (made of Cellulose; rigid outer layer) Absent
Plastids (Chloroplasts) Present (site of photosynthesis) Absent
Vacuoles Single, large central vacuole (holds sap) Multiple, small, temporary vacuoles
Shape Rigid, rectangular or box-like Flexible, round or irregular

Core Organelle Functions

1. Cell Membrane

Also called the plasma membrane, it acts as a selective barrier. It controls the entry and exit of substances like nutrients, water, and waste, protecting the internal cell environment.

2. Nucleus

Often referred to as the command center of the cell, it contains thread-like structures called chromosomes, which carry genes (DNA codes). The nucleus coordinates transcription, growth, division, and metabolic routines.

3. Cytoplasm

A jelly-like fluid that fills the cell and surrounds organelles. Most metabolic and biochemical reactions take place in the cytoplasm.

4. Mitochondria

The powerhouses of the cell. They carry out cellular respiration, utilizing oxygen to break down glucose and release energy in the form of ATP.
