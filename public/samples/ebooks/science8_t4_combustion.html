Combustion, Flame & Fuels

Studying the chemistry of burning, conditions for fire, flame structure, and fuel calorific values.

What is Combustion?

Combustion is a chemical process in which a substance reacts with oxygen to give off heat and light. The substance that undergoes combustion is called combustible or fuel.

Three Key Conditions for Combustion:

  1. Presence of a Combustible Substance: A fuel (wood, coal, gas).
  2. Presence of Supporter of Combustion: Oxygen/Air.
  3. Attainment of Ignition Temperature: The lowest temperature at which a substance catches fire.

Structure of a Candle Flame

A flame represents the zone of burning vaporized fuel. A candle flame has three distinct zones:

  • Outer Zone of Complete Combustion (Non-Luminous): Blue in color. This is the hottest part of the flame because it has access to plenty of oxygen.
  • Middle Zone of Partial Combustion (Luminous): Yellow in color. This zone contains unburnt carbon particles that glow hot. It is moderately hot.
  • Innermost Zone of Unburnt Wax Vapors: Black in color. This is the coolest part of the flame, directly surrounding the wick.

Fuel Efficiency & Calorific Value

The amount of heat energy produced on complete combustion of 1 kg of a fuel is called its calorific value. It is expressed in the unit kilojoule per kg (kJ/kg).

Fuel Type Calorific Value (kJ/kg) Evaluation
Wood 17,000 – 22,000 Low Efficiency / Polluting
Coal 25,000 – 33,000 Medium Efficiency
LPG 55,000 High Efficiency / Clean
Hydrogen 150,000 Highest Efficiency / Zero Carbon
