2. The Solar System

Journeying across orbits, planets, gravity, and the Sun.

Our Cosmic Neighborhood

Our solar system consists of our central star, the Sun, and everything bound to it by gravity—the planets Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune; dwarf planets such as Pluto; dozens of moons; and millions of asteroids, comets, and meteoroids.

Inner vs Outer Planets

Terrestrial Planets (Inner): Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars. They are made mostly of rock and metal and have solid surfaces.

Gas Giants (Outer): Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune. They are massive and composed primarily of hydrogen, helium, and water ice.

🌌 The Force of Gravity

Gravity is the invisible force that pulls objects toward each other. The Sun's massive gravitational pull keeps the planets locked in their elliptical orbits, ensuring they circle the Sun rather than drifting off into space.
