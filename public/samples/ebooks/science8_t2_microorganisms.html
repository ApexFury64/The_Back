Microorganisms: Friend or Foe

Investigating microbes, vaccines, food preservation, soil fertility, and common pathogens.

What are Microorganisms?

Microorganisms (or microbes) are tiny organisms that are invisible to the naked eye. We can only see them under a microscope. Microbes are highly adaptable and exist in all climates, from freezing glaciers to hot springs.

Major Groups of Microorganisms:

  • Bacteria: Single-celled prokaryotes (e.g. Lactobacillus, E. coli).
  • Fungi: Multicellular or unicellular saprophytes (e.g. Yeast, Bread Mould, Penicillium).
  • Protozoa: Animal-like single-celled organisms (e.g. Amoeba, Paramecium).
  • Algae: Photosynthetic aquatic organisms (e.g. Spirogyra, Chlamydomonas).
  • Viruses: Microscopic entities that reproduce only inside the host organism's cells (e.g. Influenza, COVID-19).

Friends: Friendly Microorganisms

Microbes play critical beneficial roles in our everyday lives, industries, and ecosystems:

🥣 Commercial & Food Uses

Curd: The bacterium Lactobacillus converts milk into curd.
Baking: Yeast ferments sugar in dough, releasing carbon dioxide, causing bread and cakes to rise and become spongy.

💊 Medical Uses & Vaccines

Antibiotics: Medicines made from fungi/bacteria that kill or stop pathogenic bacteria (e.g. Penicillin).
Vaccines: Dead or weakened microbes are introduced into the body to train the immune system to produce antibodies.

Foes: Harmful Pathogens

Disease-causing microorganisms are called pathogens. They infect humans, plants, and animals through water, air, food, or direct contact.

Human Disease Causative Microbe Mode of Transmission
Tuberculosis Bacteria Air
Malaria Protozoa (Plasmodium) Mosquito Bite (Female Anopheles)
Chicken Pox Virus Air / Direct Contact
