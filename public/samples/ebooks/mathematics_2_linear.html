Unit: Algebra & Equations

2. Linear Equations & Graphing

Understanding coordinate planes, lines, slopes, and y-intercepts.

What is a Linear Equation?

A linear equation is an equation for a straight line. The equation can have variables, but no variable is raised to a power higher than 1 (like x² or x³). When plotted on a graph, it forms a perfectly straight line.

Slope-Intercept Form

y = mx + c

m = slope (steepness)  |  c = y-intercept (where the line crosses the y-axis)

Finding the Slope (m)

The slope represents the "rise over run" of the line. It tells us how steep the line is and which direction it goes:

  • Positive Slope: The line goes up from left to right.
  • Negative Slope: The line goes down from left to right.
  • Zero Slope: The line is perfectly horizontal.

📍 Plotting Coordinate Points

To plot a line, start at the y-intercept (0, c). Then, use the slope m = rise / run to locate the second point. Draw a straight line connecting these points to visualize the linear graph!
