Unit: Forces, Friction & Pressures

Force and Pressure Dynamics

Exploring push/pull forces, contact vs non-contact forces, pressure equations, and atmospheric pressure.

What is a Force?

A force is a push or pull acting upon an object resulting from its interaction with another object. Forces can change:

  • The state of motion of an object (rest to motion, speed up, or slow down).
  • The direction of a moving object.
  • The shape or size of an object.

Contact vs. Non-Contact Forces:

Contact Forces: Act only when two objects physically touch (e.g. Muscular Force, Frictional Force).
Non-Contact Forces: Act from a distance without physical contact (e.g. Gravitational Force, Magnetic Force, Electrostatic Force).

Pressure

Pressure is defined as the force acting perpendicularly per unit area of a surface.

Pressure (P) = Force (F) / Area (A)
SI Unit: Pascal (Pa) or N/m²

Area Relation: If the force remains constant, pressure is inversely proportional to the area. This explains why:

  • School bags have broad straps (reduces pressure on shoulders).
  • Cutting knives have sharp edges (smaller area generates higher pressure to cut easily).
  • Railway tracks are laid on wide wooden/concrete sleepers to distribute load.

Atmospheric Pressure

The envelope of air surrounding the earth is known as the atmosphere. The pressure exerted by this air column on any surface is called atmospheric pressure.

Why aren't we crushed? The internal pressure inside our body is equal to the atmospheric pressure outside, canceling it out completely.
