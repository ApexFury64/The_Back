Stars and the Solar System

Exploring celestial bodies, constellations, moon phases, and planetary orbits.

The Moon and Its Phases

The moon is the earth's only natural satellite. It does not produce its own light, but reflects light from the sun. The day-to-day changes in the shape of the bright part of the moon as seen from the earth are called the phases of the moon.

🌑

Key Lunar Events

New Moon: The moon is completely dark as the illuminated half faces away from earth.
Full Moon: The entire illuminated side is visible from earth. The cycle takes 29.5 days.

Constellations

Stars are celestial bodies that emit their own light (like the Sun). A group of stars forming a recognizable shape or pattern is called a constellation. Some famous constellations include:

  • Ursa Major (Great Bear / Saptarishi): Shaped like a ladle or question mark, used to locate the Pole Star.
  • Orion (The Hunter): Visible in late winter, containing three bright stars in a line representing the belt.
  • Cassiopeia: Visible in early winter evenings, shaped like a distorted "W" or "M".

The Solar System

The Sun and the celestial bodies which revolve around it form the Solar System. It consists of a large number of bodies, such as planets, comets, asteroids, and meteors:

Category Planets Key Feature
Inner Planets (Terrestrial) Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars Rocky surfaces, few or no moons, close to the Sun.
Outer Planets (Gas Giants) Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune Gaseous structures, ring systems, numerous moons.
