Chapter 1.1: Introduction to Rational Numbers

Welcome to your first step in understanding Rational Numbers! In earlier classes, we have learned about different types of numbers like natural numbers, whole numbers, and integers. Let's do a quick recap:

Natural Numbers (N)

Counting numbers starting from 1, 2, 3, ...

Whole Numbers (W)

Natural numbers including zero: 0, 1, 2, 3, ...

Integers (Z)

Positive and negative whole numbers: ..., -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, ...

What is a Rational Number?

A number which can be written in the form p / q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0, is called a rational number.

Examples of Rational Numbers:

  • 5 / 8 (here p = 5, q = 8)
  • -3 / 7 (here p = -3, q = 7)
  • 0 (can be written as 0/1, so it is a rational number!)
  • -5 (can be written as -5/1)
Concept Check

Why is 5 / 0 not a rational number? Because the denominator is zero (q = 0), which is undefined in mathematics!
