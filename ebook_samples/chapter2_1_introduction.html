Chapter 2.1: Introduction to Linear Equations in One Variable

In algebra, equations are mathematical statements showing that two expressions are equal. Let's look at what makes an equation a Linear Equation in One Variable.

Key Terminology:

  • Algebraic Expression: A combination of variables, constants, and operators (e.g. 2x + 3).
  • Equation: A statement asserting the equality of two expressions (e.g. 2x + 3 = 7).
  • One Variable: The equation contains only a single variable (usually x, y, or z).
  • Linear: The highest exponent/degree of the variable is exactly 1 (e.g. x¹, not x² or x³).

Parts of an Equation

2x + 5 = 15
Left Hand Side (LHS)
Right Hand Side (RHS)

How to Solve a Linear Equation?

Solving an equation means finding the value of the variable that makes the statement true (the LHS equals the RHS). We do this by applying inverse operations to isolate the variable.

Example:

Solve: 2x - 3 = 7
1. Add 3 to both sides: 2x = 7 + 3
2. Simplify: 2x = 10
3. Divide both sides by 2: x = 10 / 2
4. Answer: x = 5
